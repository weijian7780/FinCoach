## PTPTN (National Higher Education Fund)
PTPTN is a study loan for Malaysian students in higher education. Repayment usually
begins after graduation. Borrowers can repay through salary deduction, scheduled
direct debit, or online banking. Paying consistently and early can reduce the loan
burden, and PTPTN sometimes offers discounts for early or full settlement.

## Budgeting on a student allowance
A common approach for students is to split each month's allowance: a portion for
needs (food, transport, rent), a portion for wants, and a portion for savings.
Tracking small daily expenses such as food and drinks matters, because they add up
quickly over a month and are easy to forget.

## Saving consistently as a student
Setting a specific savings goal (for example a laptop or an emergency fund) makes
saving easier than saving with no target. Even a small fixed amount saved at the
start of each month, before spending, builds the habit. An emergency fund of a few
hundred ringgit helps avoid borrowing when unexpected costs appear.

## EPF and SOCSO (for students with part-time or internship income)
EPF (KWSP) is Malaysia's retirement savings fund; contributions are deducted from
formal employment income. SOCSO (PERKESO) provides social security protection such
as employment injury coverage. Students working part-time or in internships may see
these deductions and should understand what they are for.
