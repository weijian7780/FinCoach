# EPF (KWSP) basics for Malaysian students and new workers

Official sources checked: 22 July 2026

- Employees Provident Fund (KWSP), "Mandatory Contribution":
  https://www.kwsp.gov.my/en/member/savings/mandatory-contribution
- KWSP, "i-Simpan: Contribute Voluntarily":
  https://www.kwsp.gov.my/en/member/savings/self-contribution

## Starting work and checking contributions

For work that is subject to EPF contribution rules, both employee and employer
contributions are calculated from eligible monthly wages. The current statutory
rates and wage rules must be checked on the official KWSP page because they can
change. A new worker can check credited contributions in their EPF statement via
KWSP i-Akaun and should compare it with their payslip if a deduction was made.

FinCoach must not calculate a statutory EPF contribution from memory or claim that
a particular job is covered without checking the user's work arrangement against
current official KWSP guidance.

## Voluntary retirement savings: i-Simpan

i-Simpan is KWSP's voluntary contribution option for eligible Malaysian citizens
or permanent residents who are registered EPF members. It lets a member add to
retirement savings based on their own financial capacity. The official page states
that salaried employees may contribute voluntarily in addition to mandatory
contributions, and that payments made to EPF are generally final rather than a
short-term emergency fund.

For a student, voluntary EPF saving should come only after essential expenses and
an accessible emergency buffer are considered. It is not appropriate to advise a
student to put rent, food, tuition, or debt-repayment money into a long-term
savings product.

## Coaching boundaries

Use the current KWSP site for eligibility, contribution limits, payment methods,
withdrawal rules, and any tax-relief claim. FinCoach may give budgeting education
but must not provide legal, tax, or retirement-product advice as a substitute for
KWSP or a qualified professional.
