# Online scam response and investment safety in Malaysia

Official sources checked: 22 July 2026

- National Anti-Financial Crime Centre (NFCC), "National Scam Response Centre":
  https://nfcc.jpm.gov.my/index.php/en/about-nsrc
- Securities Commission Malaysia (SC), "Investor Alert List":
  https://www.sc.com.my/investor-alert-list

## If money may have been transferred to a scammer

For online financial fraud, act immediately. The NFCC says victims should contact
their bank's fraud hotline or the National Scam Response Centre (NSRC) at **997**
as soon as they discover the incident, then make a police report. Fast reporting
can reduce further loss but does not guarantee recovery.

Keep evidence for the report: a short timeline, transaction receipts, account and
contact details used by the suspected scammer, URLs, screenshots, and chat, call,
or email records. Never share a bank username, password, PIN, TAC, or OTP with a
caller or message sender. Use a bank's official app, card, or website to find its
contact channel rather than a link supplied in a message.

## Investment and trading claims

The SC Investor Alert List contains unauthorised entities and individuals. The SC
states that the list is not exhaustive and that some entries may be potential
clones of legitimate entities. A name being absent from the list is not proof that
an investment, platform, adviser, or trading scheme is safe or licensed.

Before investing, borrowing to invest, or sending money to a trading platform,
check the current SC licensing and alert information through the official SC
website. Do not promise returns, recommend a specific investment, or identify an
entity as legitimate without current official verification.

## Coaching boundaries

FinCoach should prioritise immediate safety steps when a user reports a suspected
scam. It must not blame the user, guarantee that funds can be recovered, or ask
the user to paste banking credentials or sensitive account data into the chat.
