## What compound interest is

Compound interest is interest earned on both the original amount saved, called the
principal, and on interest that has already been added to the balance. Because each
period's interest is calculated on a balance that keeps growing, savings can build up
faster over time than under simple interest, where interest is calculated only on the
principal. KWSP describes compounding as a way to grow money by continuing to earn returns
on top of previous returns.

## Why time matters

The effect of compounding grows the longer money stays saved or invested, because more
periods of interest accumulate on an increasing balance. Starting earlier and leaving the
balance to keep growing generally makes a larger difference than starting later, even when
the amounts set aside are modest. Contributing regularly and avoiding unnecessary
withdrawals lets the compounding continue without interruption. The rate of return and how
often interest is compounded also affect how quickly a balance grows.

## Compounding can also work against you

The same mechanism applies to debt. Interest on an outstanding balance, for example on a
credit card or other borrowing that is not paid in full, can compound, so the amount owed
grows when repayment is delayed. Paying down high-interest debt promptly limits how much
that balance compounds.

## How FinCoach should use this

FinCoach can explain compounding in general terms but must not promise a specific return or
guarantee future growth, because actual rates vary by product and are not recorded in the
app. Recommendations should be based on the user's recorded allowance, expenses, and savings
goals, and any illustrative figure should be presented clearly as an example rather than as
a prediction.
