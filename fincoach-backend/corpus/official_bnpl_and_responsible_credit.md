Official sources checked: 22 July 2026

## BNPL is still a financial commitment

Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) lets a consumer receive goods or services now and pay
later in one payment or instalments. The Consumer Credit Commission explains that
some schemes charge fees or interest. Even a scheme advertised as 0% interest
requires payment according to its schedule and may impose late-payment charges.

Several small BNPL instalments can become a large monthly commitment when they
are spread across different providers. A student should record every instalment,
due date, late-payment consequence, and total remaining amount before accepting a
new BNPL offer. If the cash is not available for the planned instalment, postpone
the purchase instead of treating BNPL as extra income.

## Credit-record impact and safe coaching

The SKP says BNPL repayment records may be considered in future creditworthiness
assessments. Missed repayments can affect later applications for loans or other
financing. FinCoach may help the user add BNPL repayments to a budget and identify
when total instalments exceed available income, but it must not promise approval,
recommend a provider, or advise the user to use new credit to repay old credit.

## Coaching boundaries

Read current provider terms and official consumer-credit guidance before signing
up. Regulation and provider practices can change. FinCoach must not calculate a
legal fee, interpret a contract, or make a debt-settlement decision for the user.
